# Chapter 2 Journal Entry

![Filing Cabinet](https://media.officedepot.com/images/f_auto,q_auto,e_sharpen,h_450/products/940511/940511_o01/940511)

This chapter was intriguing because it really dives deep into and explores human psychology, emotions, behavior, and thinking (subconscious and conscious) to examine how they shape how we interact with objects and use them. When people use or interact with something, they fall into two gulfs between one's desired outcome and available options. The first gulf, which is the Gulf of Execution, deals with how and where people try to figure out how it operates, while the second gulf, which is the Gulf of Evaluation, concerns how and where people try to figure out what happened. The filing cabinet example, which is on pages 37 to 39, exemplified this to me, as the Gulf of Execution was shown with the visible elements of the filing cabinet, such as the drawing handle and the slider on the handle. This then led into the Gulf of Evaluation, which was shown when Norman tried to use the drawing handle and then the slider on the handle to open the filing cabinet, and when that didn't work, he tried twisting and then pulling it to no avail. The filing cabinet, in the end, is designed poorly, and the two gulfs are present for many things, but people tend to blame themselves or give up when they experience difficulties using things and objects and other stuff, even when the difficulties are in the thing's or object's design, not in the people trying to use them. According to Norman, a designer's main objective around these gulfs is to connect them; as he says on page 38, *"The role of the designer is to help people bridge the two gulfs."*
