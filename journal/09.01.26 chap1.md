# Chapter 1 Journal Entry

[](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/61JIKsXciTL._AC_SX679_.jpg)

This chapter was interesting to read because it gave me new perspectives and nuances on how people approach designing everyday objects and items - and especially how designing them doesn't take into account people's behavior and psychology. His talk about the doors, which was on pages 1 to 3, exemplified that to me, as I was reminded of how sometimes I would come face-to-face with an unfamiliar door, and I would struggle on figuring out how to open it due to a variety of factors such as the design, lack of signs/visual aids such as 'Push' or 'Pull', or myself perceiving the design as unusual or unique. However, to some designers - and especially for engineers who helped design some of the items - even though they designed a working, functional, useful door, they failed to design an actual product that can be easily used and understood on many levels because they think the design is straightforward, yet they have not thought anything about or accounted for human behavior, psychology, and their understanding of people at all. On page 6, Norman explains why they do so, saying, *"Engineers are trained to think logically. As a result, they come to believe that all people must think this way, and they design their machines accordingly. When people have trouble, the engineers are upset, but often for the wrong reason. “What are these people doing?” they will wonder. “Why are they doing that?” The problem with the designs of most engineers is that they are too logical. We have to accept human behavior the way it is, not the way we would wish it to be."*
